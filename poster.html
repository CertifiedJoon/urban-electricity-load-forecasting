Probabilistic Electricity Load
Forecasting for Urban Buildings

Joonyoung Moon (3035666657)  |  Supervisor: Prof. Y. Wang
1. The Volatility Problem
  • Unaggregated Demand: Unlike aggregate municipal grids, individual residential load is dominated by erratic human behavior and sudden HVAC capacity spikes.
  • Predicting these profiles requires transitioning from deterministic points to robust probabilistic bounds.
2. The Mean-Prediction Trap

Standard Pinball Loss evaluates probabilistic boundaries using a strict linear multiplier:

\[ L_{\tau}(y, \hat{q}_{\tau}) = \max\big(\tau(y - \hat{q}_{\tau}), (\tau - 1)(y - \hat{q}_{\tau})\big) \]
  • Linear Dilution: Accute HVAC spike gradients are mathematically overwhelmed by prolonged blocks of low-variance normal usage.
  • The model structurally drifts toward the median baseline, severely breaking its upper-bound projections.
3. Algorithmic Interventions

We resolved this by injecting Asymmetric Spike Quantile Loss alongside partitioned data mechanics:

\[ \tilde L_{j,\tau} = \Gamma_{j,\tau} L_{\tau} \quad \Big| \quad \Gamma = \max\Big(1 + C^{\text{peak}} W_p M^{\text{peak}}, \dots\Big) \] \[ L_{\text{asym}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal N|}\sum_{\mathcal N}\tilde L_N + \frac{1}{|\mathcal E|}\sum_{\mathcal E}\tilde L_E \]
  • Compounding Penalty (Γ): Aggressively multiplies gradients strictly when a boundary fails during an extreme event.
  • Split-Aware Stratified Sampling: Artificially guarantees exposure to extremes during batching to feed the loss.
